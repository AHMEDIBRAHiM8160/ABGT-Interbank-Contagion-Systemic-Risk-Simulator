Research background

Mathematical Modelling and Risk Management in Financial Networks

Ahmed Ibrahim · U1/21/MTH/1420 · B.Sc. (Hons.) Mathematics · Department of Mathematics, Umaru Musa Yar'adua University, Katsina State, Nigeria · January 2026

This application was developed from that undergraduate research on systemic risk, interbank contagion and financial networks. Every clearing equation implemented in the engine is taken verbatim from Chapter Three of the project; the Chapter Four numerical example is reproduced as a built-in test case.

Aim

To develop and analyse a purely mathematical framework, based on the Eisenberg–Noe, Rogers–Veraart and Kusnetsov–Veraart models, for examining contagion dynamics and systemic risk in financial networks.

Objectives

  1. Formulate a rigorous mathematical representation of contagion dynamics in financial networks.
  2. Analyse the effects of interbank exposures and capital buffers on default propagation.
  3. Study equilibrium clearing payments using fixed-point and comparative static analysis.
  4. Examine how liquidity losses and fire-sale mechanisms amplify financial distress.
  5. Evaluate the effectiveness of capital buffers and exposure constraints in mitigating systemic risk.

Statement of the problem

Traditional risk models treat institutions in isolation and fail to capture risk propagation from network interdependencies; network models often simplify liquidity constraints, default costs and market feedback. The study addresses this through the fixed point P = min{L1, e + LᵀP}.

Mathematical specification implemented

Assumptions stated in the research

  • Interbank obligations are fixed and known at the initial time.
  • Institutions do not engage in strategic default.
  • The system is analysed over a single settlement period.
  • All model parameters are deterministic.
  • Limited liability — institutions cannot pay more than their available assets.
  • Proportional repayment — creditors are paid proportionally in default.

Scope limitations stated in the research

The analysis is theoretical, uses stylised networks and simulated scenarios rather than real-time data, and the Kusnetsov–Veraart specification is a reduced form that links asset depletion to payment shortfalls without explicit price-impact functions.

Points where the research text is unclear — flagged, not silently patched

  • Chapter 4 Rogers–Veraart figures. With the Chapter 4 data (e = 50, 40, 30) and α = 0.7, β = 0.9, the equation p = min{p̄, αe + βΠᵀp} yields p* = (30, 15, 20) — no default — because αe₁ = 35 already exceeds p̄₁ = 30. The reported p* = (25.4, 13.2, 17.6) therefore cannot be reproduced from that equation with an unshocked asset vector. The engine computes the equation as written and does not hard-code the reported figures. Applying an initial shock to e does produce partial defaults, consistent with the chapter's interpretation.
  • Fire-sale figures are consistent. Given p* = (25.4, 13.2, 17.6) and λ = 0.2, the equation e′ᵢ = eᵢ − λᵢ(p̄ᵢ − pᵢ)⁺ reproduces the reported e′ = (49.1, 39.6, 29.5) exactly. This is a passing test in the verification suite.
  • No shock operator is defined in the research; the shock mechanisms in this tool are labelled implementation assumptions.
  • β in the coupled system. Sec. 3.6 writes Πᵀp without β while Sec. 3.4 includes it. Both variants are selectable in the Simulation panel.
  • External liabilities do not appear in the research clearing equations; when supplied they are reported but excluded from the fixed point.
